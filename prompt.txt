How the numbers are calculated
Treasury bills are sold at a discount. You choose the face value you will receive at maturity, then pay a lower price today. CBK publishes price per KES 100 from the quoted annual yield:

P = 100 ÷ (1 + r × d ÷ 365)

r is the annual yield as a decimal (8.7837% is 0.087837).
d is 91, 182, or 364 days. The year basis is 365, matching CBK.
Bill price = P × (face value ÷ 100). P is rounded to four decimal places, matching CBK result sheets (for example 8.7674% for 91 days on 14 September 2026 priced at 97.8609).
Gross interest = face value − bill price.
Withholding tax, when included, is 15% of that discount and is added to the amount you pay CBK at settlement. You still receive full face value at maturity.
Period return uses cash actually invested (CBK payment plus extra expenses), not face value. Simple annualized return scales that by 365 ÷ days. Compounded annual return is (1 + period return)^(365 ÷ days) − 1.
The quoted yield is higher than your after-tax return because tax is paid up front on the discount.

Investor facts
When do I bid and pay?
Bids are due by 2:00pm on auction day, usually Thursday, through DhowCSD or Treasury Mobile Direct. If you are successful, pay by 2:00pm on the value date — typically the following Monday, or the next business day after a holiday.
Non-competitive or competitive?
From KES 50,000 up to just under KES 2,000,000 you bid non-competitively and take the weighted average accepted rate. From KES 2,000,000 you may also bid a yield competitively.
What number should match DhowCSD?
Match Pay CBK today (bill price plus WHT). Bank KEPSS/RTGS charges sit on top of that and are your bank’s fees, not CBK’s settlement amount.
Why is tax added to what I pay?
The bill is discounted. CBK withholds tax on that discount at settlement, so you fund price + tax now and still receive full face value at maturity.

Calculator guide

Use the tool with confidence
This page explains how to interpret what the calculator shows so you can compare bids, settlement, and final return without second-guessing the numbers.

Quick checklist
1. Pick a face value

2. Choose the tenor

3. Input the auction or bid rate

4. Add expenses and tax settings

How to read the results
Face value: This is the amount you receive at maturity. For non-competitive bids, the minimum is KES 50,000 and increments are KES 50,000. Smaller competitive bids are allowed only above KES 2,000,000.
Bill price: CBK sells the bill at a discount, so you pay less than face value today. The calculator shows the discounted amount for your selected face value.
Tax: WHT is charged on the discount and added to the amount due at settlement in the usual case of resident individual investors. Toggle tax off only if you have a valid exemption status.
Cash out: The total cash out includes the CBK payment plus any bank fees or transfer charges you add manually. That is the number to compare to your available cash.
Net profit: This is your final gain after tax and fees. It is often lower than the quoted yield because the quoted annual yield is before tax and before some execution costs.
Best practices
Use the published weighted average rates as a planning benchmark, but remember your final auction result can differ. If you are comparing three tenors, be sure to keep the same face value and tax assumptions to make the comparison fair.

Typical workflow
Plan the amount: Start with the face value you want to receive at maturity.
Select your tenor: Use 91, 182, or 364 days depending on your cash-flow timing.
Load the rate: Use the latest auction preview or your own competitive yield.
Add costs: Include bank and broker charges if they apply and mark VAT where needed.
Review final return: Check the “net profit after charges” and annualized return before you commit.
Important
The published rate is a benchmark, not a guarantee. Your actual auction allocation may differ after the competitive bidding process.

Settlement
Settlement usually falls on the next business day after the Monday following the auction. This tool reflects that rhythm to help you plan before you bid.

Tax status
Use the tax toggle carefully. Resident individuals commonly pay final WHT on the discount, but entities and exempt investors may not pay the same rate.
